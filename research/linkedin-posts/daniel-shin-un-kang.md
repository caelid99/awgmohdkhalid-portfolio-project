Is Y Combinator's "Make something people want" outdated? B2C was for consumers, and B2B was for businesses. B2A is for the third customer: Agents.

As a YC alum, I love the mantra. Marketing, sales, and product were all correctly calibrated to a human psychology - both passions like crossing off a dream bucket list, and irrationalities like FOMO or impulse buy.

Now there is a new type of customer that doesn't fit any customer segment: AI agents. They have their own parallel journey - often shared with people. Your brand can be visible to every person and business on earth and still be invisible to the systems that are building the shortlist.

At Expedia Group, we're building for it. During our flagship conference Explore a few weeks ago, Jochen Koedijk officially announced our work in Business to Agent (B2A): to study, influence, and build for these new customers.

The brands that win this era won't choose between human marketing and agent marketing. They'll build for both.

So YC is still right in spirit, but my own twist would be to "make something customers want" - humans and machines.

I'm leading this work and actively building a team for us to become the number one agent marketers in the category. DM me.

## Post 2 — 9 June 2026 — 73 likes, 11 comments — HIGH SIGNAL

URL: https://www.linkedin.com/posts/itsdankang_principal-product-manager-answer-engine-activity-7470127624770514946-pqwj

My take on AEO: It's easy to be visible to AI. It's just hard to be good.

Every consumer brand is looking for a "silver bullet" to win Answer Engine Optimization (AEO). As the channel owner at Expedia Group, my view is that there is not a single bullet that solves all your problems.

Ben Horowitz at Andreessen Horowitz has the better frame: you win with a lot of lead bullets, not one silver bullet. In AEO, no single agency, content type, file type, or technical trick makes or breaks you - or at least not in a way that lasts.

Every LLM is ultimately trying to serve its users the most relevant, useful answer. They're not perfect at it yet, but they'll get really good. So over time, winning here comes down to two things.

First, systems to produce and optimize a content portfolio that stays legible and useful to agents. Band-aid solutions that are reactive to algorithm changes are not enough and do not scale.

Second, the ground truth of real value. Agents will get better at evaluating the actual offering, not just the page or .txt wrapped around it. No amount of optimization fixes a weak product or a weak content portfolio.

AEO is the fastest-growing channel at Expedia Group, and we're helping travelers navigate one of the most complex and high-consideration categories in the world. I'm hiring for a Principal to scale this channel with me directly.

Which means people just lost their smartest coworker.

Agents work for you in your sleep and multiply your productivity. That's why I think of AI as the fourth form of leverage, alongside labor, capital, and software.

What's strange is that I assumed AI leverage is like software, but it seems to behave much closer to labor. When an LLM goes down, it doesn't feel like a tool failing. It feels like your best work colleague didn't show up.

And we're still early. Today it's mostly developers who are annoyed.
But imagine an agent-first world where everyone relies on:
- finance agents
- legal agents
- travel agents
- research agents

When that system goes down, it won't feel like software failing. It will feel like part of the workforce just disappeared.

The real AI infrastructure problem hasn't started yet.
